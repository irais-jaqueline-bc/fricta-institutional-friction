# Theoretical Framework: FRICTA Model

## 1. Introduction

The FRICTA Project (Friction in Childcare Technology Adoption) investigates digital adoption friction in Mexican childcare institutions. Digital adoption in this sector is not treated as a purely technological process, but as a multidimensional institutional phenomenon shaped by infrastructure, organizational capacity, operational workload, human readiness, and resistance to change.

FRICTA argues that focusing only on hardware or connectivity provides an incomplete explanation of digital adoption outcomes. In childcare institutions, digital systems can fail not because technology is absent, but because institutional conditions do not support sustainable integration into daily operations.

Within this project, digital adoption friction is understood as the structural, operational, and cognitive resistance generated when the demands of digital tools exceed the institution’s available capacity, resources, organization, training, or operational flexibility.

---

## 2. Technology–Organization–Environment Framework

The Technology–Organization–Environment (TOE) framework provides the main structural foundation for FRICTA. TOE proposes that technological adoption is shaped by technological, organizational, and environmental conditions.

In FRICTA, the technological context is represented by infrastructure-related variables such as device availability, internet stability, and existing digital tool use. These variables capture whether the institution has the minimum technical conditions required for digital adoption.

The organizational context is represented by variables related to administrative organization, implementation difficulty, staffing limitations, operational workload, and prior digital exposure. These variables capture whether the institution has the internal structure and capacity needed to integrate digital tools sustainably.

The environmental or institutional context is represented through contextual metadata such as institution type, state, and institutional scale. These variables are not treated as direct friction indicators, but as contextual factors that may help explain variation across institutions.

TOE supports FRICTA’s central assumption that digital adoption cannot be explained by infrastructure alone; it depends on the interaction between technical conditions and organizational capacity.

---

## 3. Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology

The Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) provides the behavioral and adoption-readiness foundation for FRICTA. UTAUT integrates concepts such as performance expectancy, effort expectancy, facilitating conditions, and behavioral intention.

Within FRICTA, UTAUT supports the inclusion of variables related to perceived usefulness, willingness to adopt digital tools, digital usage frequency, and training limitations.

These variables help capture whether institutional actors perceive digital tools as useful, feasible, and adoptable within their current operational conditions.

FRICTA does not treat human adoption readiness as independent from institutional context. Instead, adoption readiness is interpreted as conditioned by infrastructure, organization, workload, and available support.

---

## 4. Cognitive Load and Administrative Burden

FRICTA incorporates cognitive load and administrative burden perspectives to explain why digital tools may increase friction when institutions are already operationally saturated.

In childcare institutions, staff members often manage administrative duties, caregiving responsibilities, emergencies, and reporting obligations simultaneously. Under these conditions, even useful digital tools may become difficult to adopt if they increase learning demands, time pressure, or procedural complexity.

This theoretical perspective supports the inclusion of variables such as administrative time load, time scarcity, staffing scarcity, implementation difficulty, and training deficit.

Operational load is therefore treated as a central component of digital adoption friction, not as background noise.

---

## 5. Organizational Inertia and Resistance to Change

FRICTA also draws from organizational inertia and resistance-to-change literature. Digital friction may emerge not only from the absence of technology, but from the cost of changing existing routines, records, workflows, and decision structures.

In this project, resistance to change is represented through variables such as system change resistance, implementation difficulty, administrative disorganization, and prior digital implementation exposure.

This perspective allows FRICTA to distinguish between institutions that lack infrastructure and institutions that have some technological resources but still face organizational barriers to implementation.

---

## 6. Binding Constraints and Institutional Prioritization

FRICTA uses binding-constraint logic as a conceptual basis for prioritizing institutional barriers. The central idea is that adoption failure may be driven by a small number of constraints that limit the effectiveness of all other improvements.

For example, training may have limited effect if the institution lacks stable connectivity; similarly, infrastructure may have limited effect if organizational processes are too fragmented to absorb digital change.

This logic supports FRICTA’s multidimensional structure and motivates the comparison between equal-weight and theoretically informed scoring scenarios in the metrics methodology.

---

## 7. Conceptual Definition of Digital Adoption Friction

Within this research, digital adoption friction is defined as:

> The structural, operational, and cognitive resistance generated by the mismatch between digital system requirements and the institutional capacity required to implement, integrate, and sustain those systems in childcare organizations.

FRICTA conceptualizes friction as a gradient rather than a binary condition. Institutions may experience low, moderate, high, or severe friction depending on the interaction between infrastructural readiness, organizational constraints, operational load, and human adoption readiness.

---

## 8. Theoretical Premise

FRICTA operates under the premise that digital adoption friction emerges from the interaction between infrastructural readiness, organizational capacity, operational saturation, and human adoption readiness.

While infrastructure is a necessary enabling condition, it is not sufficient to explain sustainable adoption. Institutions with adequate connectivity or devices may still experience high digital adoption friction if they face administrative overload, staffing limitations, resistance to change, fragmented workflows, or insufficient training.

This theoretical premise supports FRICTA as a multidimensional institutional diagnostic framework rather than a simple technology-access survey.
