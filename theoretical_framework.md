# Theoretical Framework: FRICTA Model

## 1. Introduction
The **FRICTA Project** (Friction in Childcare Technology Adoption) investigates the digital transformation of childcare institutions. Digital adoption in this sector is a multi-dimensional phenomenon; it is not merely a matter of purchasing hardware, but a process influenced by **organizational capacity**, **administrative structures**, and **institutional readiness**.

FRICTA argues that focusing solely on infrastructure (connectivity and hardware) provides an incomplete picture. To understand why some institutions successfully integrate digital systems while others struggle, it is necessary to analyze the friction generated by the interaction between operational capacity, resource constraints, administrative workload, and perceived utility.

FRICTA does not assume that technological adoption is universally beneficial; rather, it investigates the conditions under which digital tools become operationally sustainable within childcare institutions.

---

## 2. Core Frameworks & Theoretical Pillars

###  Pillar 1: The TOE Framework (Technology–Organization–Environment)

Developed by Tornatzky and Fleischer, the TOE Framework proposes that technological adoption depends on three contextual dimensions. FRICTA adapts these dimensions to the operational reality of childcare institutions.

* **Technological Context:** Availability and quality of technological resources (e.g., `computer_access`, `internet_stability`).
* **Organizational Context:** Internal institutional characteristics such as staffing, structure, workload, and administrative organization (e.g., `staff_size`, `process_organization_level`).
* **Environmental / Institutional Context:** External conditions shaping institutional operation (e.g., `state`, `institution_type`).

This framework supports the idea that digital adoption cannot be explained exclusively through infrastructure availability.

---

###  Pillar 2: TAM (Technology Acceptance Model)

The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) explains adoption through two major dimensions:

* **Perceived Usefulness (PU):** The extent to which a technology is perceived as beneficial (`perceived_usefulness`).
* **Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU):** The extent to which implementation and usage are perceived as manageable (`switching_difficulty`, `implementation_difficulty`).

Within FRICTA, TAM helps explain institutional openness toward adopting new digital tools.

---

###  Pillar 3: Organizational Readiness for Change

Research on organizational readiness suggests that successful technological transformation depends on institutional preparedness, available capacity, and operational flexibility.

In nonprofit and socially vulnerable sectors, organizational readiness is often constrained by:
* limited staff,
* administrative overload,
* insufficient training,
* and limited operational flexibility.

FRICTA applies this framework to variables such as:
* `lack_of_training`
* `lack_of_personnel`
* `admin_time_load`
* `process_organization_level`

This framework supports the hypothesis that organizational conditions may influence adoption more strongly than infrastructure alone.

---

###  Pillar 4: Resistance to Change & Switching Costs

Technological friction is not always caused by the absence of tools; it can also emerge from the operational cost of abandoning existing systems.

This framework justifies the inclusion of:
* `previous_adoption_attempt`
* `switching_difficulty`
* `implementation_difficulty`

These variables help measure institutional resistance associated with transition costs, operational disruption, and implementation burden.

## 4. Conceptual Definition of FRICTA

Within this research, **Digital Adoption Friction** is defined as:

> *"The structural and operational resistance generated by the mismatch between technological demands and the institutional capacity (time, personnel, organization, and training) required to sustainably integrate digital systems within childcare institutions."*

FRICTA conceptualizes friction not as a binary condition, but as a gradient representing the degree of difficulty institutions face when attempting to integrate and sustain digital tools within contemporary operational conditions.

---

## 5. Hypothesis Justification

Based on the integration of the TOE Framework and Organizational Readiness literature, FRICTA operates under the following theoretical premise:

> **Organizational and readiness-related variables will exert a stronger influence on digital adoption friction than technical infrastructure variables alone.**

While infrastructure factors such as `internet_stability` and `computer_access` are necessary conditions for adoption, they may not be sufficient predictors of sustainable implementation.

FRICTA expects organizational constraints — particularly administrative workload (`admin_time_load`), lack of personnel (`lack_of_personnel`), and insufficient training (`lack_of_training`) — to emerge as stronger predictors of adoption friction than infrastructure availability by itself.

The project therefore hypothesizes that institutions with relatively adequate infrastructure may still experience high levels of digital adoption friction when organizational readiness remains limited.
